Data leakage checks

How published models inflate their metrics using data unavailable at 11:00 PM, and the exact unit tests built to prevent them.

1. Blank Tail Numbers on Cancellations (BTS Reporting Artifact)

When an airline cancels a flight, they leave the plane's tail registration number blank in the BTS database. In the 2.5-year dataset, 931 flights had blank tail IDs, and 100% of them were cancelled. Models with Tail_Number.isna() achieve near 1.00 ROC-AUC by memorizing this post-hoc clerical artifact.

Fix (DL-021): Purged blank tail indicators from training. LightGBM trains exclusively on flights with known tail assignments; still predicts all rows at runtime.

2. Realized Aircraft Swaps (`chain_break`)

BTS records the physical plane that actually flew, not the plane planned yesterday. When a plane breaks down, dispatchers swap aircraft. In the data, the plane's previous destination doesn't match tomorrow's origin. These flights had a 24.3% cancellation and 40.7% delay rate (vs 1.1% and 20.0% normally).

Fix: Removed chain_break features and rebuilt the Markov rotation graph so B4v2 never resets chains on post-hoc swaps.

3. Cheating on Forecast Horizon (The 2-Hour Window)

Many Kaggle kernels predict delays 2 hours prior or at gate pushback using active taxi-out times, gate delay announcements, and inbound airborne telemetry. That is a tactical radar problem, not a night-before strategic forecast.

Fix: Locked cutoff at exactly 23:00 ET on Day D-1. Features use strictly zero data created after this cutoff.

4. Observed Realized Weather (METAR)

Papers feed actual observed weather (e.g. rain intensity, measured visibility) at takeoff time. At 11:00 PM the night before, tomorrow's actual weather does not exist.

Fix: Strictly integrated point-in-time forecasts (TAF, NBM, SPC) issued before 22:30 ET. Audited 527,415 flights with 0 late products.

5. Random Train/Test Splits (Time-Travel Autocorrelation)

Using train_test_split(shuffle=True) randomly scatters morning and afternoon flights from the same day across train and test sets. The model memorizes that day's nationwide ATC ground stop to predict the test flights.

Fix: Enforced a 2.5-year walk-forward backtest (2023–2025). Models train only on strictly past dates.

Project overview

Use these structured talking points to explain the problem, architecture, and defensibility in technical interviews.

S Situation (The Problem)
  • Air travelers must make decisions at 11:00 PM the night before departure (set alarms, adjust meetings).
  • Airlines almost never post delay notifications that early.
  • Two hours before departure, delay prediction is trivial because inbound planes are already late.
  • At 11:00 PM, airplanes are three cities away, crews are resting, and no live radar signal exists.
  • Published papers claiming 90% accuracy frequently cheat by using data recorded after landing.
T Task (The Engineering Objective)
  • Build an honest machine learning pipeline to forecast next-day flight delays for Raleigh-Durham (RDU).
  • Enforce an absolute point-in-time boundary: zero data created after 11:00 PM on day D-1.
  • Predict calibrated probability distributions across five delay buckets (on-time to cancelled).
  • Output continuous delay minutes across percentiles (5% to 95%).
  • Build automated test suites to catch and ban any form of future data leakage.
A Analysis (What Was Built)
  • Audited 1.48 million BTS flights and caught clerical cheats (blank tail numbers on cancellations).
  • Stitched multi-hop aircraft rotations to track turnaround padding across national airframes.
  • Integrated 77 point-in-time weather forecast features (TAF, NBM, SPC) frozen at 10:30 PM.
  • Engineered overnight positioning features tracking where the plane slept and yesterday's arrival delay.
  • Evaluated across a 2.5-year walk-forward backtest (37,087 flights, 2023–2025).
  • Built 17 automated unit and walk-forward leakage tests (future-scramble, canary, shuffled labels).
R Result (The Validated Impact)
  • Probability error (Brier score) dropped by 15.2% over climatology (0.1614 down to 0.1368).
  • Delay minute error (CRPS) fell from 13.29 minutes down to 11.34 minutes per flight (+14.7% skill over Markov).
  • Physical split: overnight positioning cut 6:00 AM departure error by 0.97m; weather cut evening error by 1.67m.
  • Near-perfect calibration: 80% prediction interval captured exactly 80.18% of real flights (ECE = 0.00800).
  • All 17 automated tests passed, establishing a verified, leak-free open aviation benchmark.
